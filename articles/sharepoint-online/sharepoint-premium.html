SharePoint Premium (formerly Syntex) – Document Processing, Autofill Columns and Content AI

The AI content services of SharePoint, covering prebuilt and custom document processing models, autofill columns, taxonomy tagging, content assembly, eSignature, translation, OCR, pay-as-you-go billing, and where it fits next to Copilot.

SharePoint Premium (the evolution of Project Cortex → SharePoint Syntex, renamed in 2023) adds AI services that understand documents: extracting fields, classifying files, generating documents from templates and more. Most features are pay-as-you-go through an Azure subscription linked in the Microsoft 365 admin center.

#Capabilities

#How document processing works

Model type Best for Training
Prebuilt (invoice, receipt, contract, ID…) Common business documents None (ready to use)
Unstructured (teaching method) Classification + extraction from varied text (contracts, letters) ~5 positive + 1 negative examples, explanations
Freeform / Structured (AI Builder based) Forms with consistent layout or free text Label samples in AI Builder
Autofill columns (LLM) Quick extraction or summaries with natural language prompts None, just a prompt per column

#Premium vs Copilot

SharePoint Premium Microsoft 365 Copilot
Purpose Turn content into structured metadata, automate processing Help users ask, summarize and create
Output Columns, content types, generated docs Answers, drafts, summaries
Runs On upload/update (background) On demand, per user
Licensing Pay-as-you-go Per-user licence

They complement each other. Better metadata from Premium leads to better Copilot answers and agent grounding.

#Interview questions

Q1What is the difference between prebuilt and custom document processing models?

Prebuilt models are trained by Microsoft for common document types (invoices, receipts, contracts) and work immediately. Custom models (unstructured, freeform, structured) are trained by your organization on sample documents to classify and extract organization-specific information.

Q2How are autofill columns different from traditional extraction models?

Autofill columns use a large language model with a natural-language prompt per column ("summarize the scope of work in two sentences"), so no training examples are needed. They are best for summaries and flexible extraction, while trained models give more deterministic results for high-volume structured processing.
