Technical Lead & Solution Architect Interview Questions

Questions for senior roles, covering architecture trade-offs, platform selection, governance, ALM, estimation, stakeholder management, team leadership, transitions and knowledge transfer, quality and risk, with model answers.

#Architecture & platform

Q1How do you choose between SharePoint lists, Dataverse and Azure SQL?

Assess data complexity (relations), volume, security granularity, audit needs, user licensing, who builds and maintains it, and integration needs. Lists suit simple M365-licensed apps, Dataverse suits relational and secure Power Platform apps, and SQL suits existing or reporting-heavy LOB data behind APIs. Record the decision in an ADR.

Q2How do you decide between Power Apps, SPFx and a custom web app?

Audience and licensing, UX and performance requirements, team skills, ALM expectations, and where the experience lives (SharePoint pages, Teams, standalone). Mixed solutions are common: Power Apps for data entry, SPFx for intranet UX, APIs for logic.

Q3What non-functional requirements do you always check?

Security and compliance, identity, performance and scale (thresholds, delegation), availability, accessibility, monitoring, licensing, data residency, retention, supportability and ALM.

Q4How do you design for SharePoint Online throttling?

Minimize calls (delta, batching, selects), use app identities per workload, back off with Retry-After, decorate traffic, schedule heavy jobs off-peak, and use the Migration API for bulk content.

Q5What's your approach to security design?

Least privilege (Sites.Selected, scoped roles), managed identities and Key Vault, no secrets in clients, group-based access, labels and DLP, threat modelling for integrations, and access reviews.

#Delivery & governance

Q6How do you set up ALM for a Power Platform + SPFx program?

Environments (dev, test, prod) with managed solutions, environment variables and connection references, Git source control (unpacked solutions and SPFx repos), pipelines (Azure DevOps or GitHub), solution checker, approvals and release notes.

Q7How do you estimate a migration project?

Assessment-driven: volumes, complexity classes per site, customizations rebuild effort, pilot throughput measurements, wave plan, plus testing, communication, hypercare and contingency based on risks.

Q8How do you handle scope creep?

A signed BRD baseline, a change request process with impact analysis (scope, cost, timeline), backlog prioritization with the product owner, and transparent trade-offs.

Q9How do you govern citizen development?

Environment strategy, DLP, Managed Environments, a CoE kit inventory, maker onboarding, promotion paths for business-critical apps into managed ALM, and templates and components.

Q10How do you measure solution success?

Agree KPIs up front (cycle time, adoption, errors, audit findings), instrument (analytics, Application Insights, Power BI), and review post-go-live with stakeholders.

#Leadership

Q11How do you lead a team supporting 50+ SharePoint applications?

Inventory and criticality, SUDs and runbooks, on-call and escalation matrix, knowledge-sharing rotations, recurring problem management, and automation of repetitive fixes, plus clear SLAs and metrics.

Q12How do you run a transition from another vendor?

Plan KT phases (docs review → shadow → reverse shadow → independent), create or validate SUDs, access and environment readiness, risk log, and sign-off criteria per application.

Q13How do you mentor developers in a mixed low-code/pro-code team?

Pairing, code and solution reviews, coding standards (Power Fx, flows, SPFx), reusable components, brown-bag sessions, and clear guidance on when to choose which tool.

Q14Tell us about a difficult stakeholder situation.

Use STAR: describe the context (e.g. a demand to migrate everything as-is), your actions (data-driven ROT analysis, options with cost and risk), and the result (archive strategy approved, timeline met). Show empathy and evidence.

Q15How do you handle a production incident as lead?

Stabilize first (communicate, mitigate), then investigate the root cause with logs and timeline, fix and verify, then run a blameless post-mortem with preventive actions (monitoring, tests, runbook updates).

Q16How do you balance speed and quality in low-code projects?

Guardrails over gates: templates, components, environment strategy and automated checks (solution checker, linting), lightweight reviews, and investment in reuse.

Q17How do you keep up with Microsoft 365 changes?

Message Center and roadmap, PnP community calls, release notes (SPFx, Power Platform), test tenants or early release rings, and a team knowledge base.

Q18How would you introduce Copilot safely?

Readiness assessment (oversharing, labels, content hygiene), restricted access or discovery for sensitive sites, a pilot with champions, governance for agents, training, and metrics.

Q19What's your view on technical debt in SharePoint estates?

Inventory legacy dependencies (InfoPath, workflows, add-ins, farm code), prioritize by risk and retirement dates, budget modernization alongside features, and prevent new debt with standards.

Q20Why should we hire you as a lead?

Connect your experience: delivery across on-prem and Online, Power Platform, Nintex migration, SPFx with CI/CD, leading teams and transitions, plus measurable outcomes and a structured, communicative approach.
