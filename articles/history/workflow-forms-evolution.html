The Evolution of Workflows & Forms – From SPD and InfoPath to Power Platform, Nintex NAC and Agents

Twenty years of business process automation on SharePoint, covering 2007/2010 workflows, SharePoint Designer, InfoPath, Nintex, 2013 Workflow Manager, Power Automate and Power Apps, NAC, and today's AI agents, with the reasons for each shift.

  1. 2003Forms

    InfoPath 2003

    XML forms designer; desktop forms submitted to SharePoint libraries.

  2. 2006–07Workflow

    MOSS 2007 workflows + InfoPath Forms Services

    Approval workflows on WF 3.0; browser forms; SharePoint Designer 2007 workflows.

  3. 2007–08Ecosystem

    Nintex Workflow 2007

    Browser drag-and-drop workflows become hugely popular.

  4. 2010Workflow

    SPD 2010 reusable workflows, InfoPath 2010 list forms

    Task processes, impersonation step; customizing list forms with InfoPath.

  5. 2012Ecosystem

    Nintex Forms

    Friendlier forms designer with mobile layouts.

  6. 2013Workflow

    Workflow Manager (2013 platform)

    Stage-based workflows with loops and REST calls; InfoPath 2013 is the last version.

  7. 2016Cloud

    Microsoft Flow & PowerApps

    Cloud automation and low-code apps; customized SharePoint list forms with PowerApps (2017–18).

  8. 2019Cloud

    Power Automate

    Flow renamed; RPA added.

  9. 2020Retirement

    2010 workflows retired in SPO

    Push to Power Automate.

  10. 2022Ecosystem

    Nintex Automation Cloud

    Nintex's cloud platform for forms, workflows and documents.

  11. 2026Retirement

    2013 workflows, add-ins & InfoPath retiring

    Power Platform, NAC and SPFx form customizers become the standard; AI agents automate triage and assistance.

#Interview questions

Q1Why did InfoPath decline?

Microsoft stopped investing after InfoPath 2013 (announced 2014). Its desktop-centric design, XML storage and code-behind didn't fit cloud and mobile, and Power Apps, Nintex and SPFx offered modern alternatives.

Q2What drove organizations from SPD workflows to Power Automate?

Retirement of 2010 and 2013 workflows in SharePoint Online, the connector ecosystem beyond SharePoint, modern approvals in Teams and Outlook, better monitoring and ALM, and Microsoft's investment focus.
